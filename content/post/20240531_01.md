# Questions I need answers to
+ Does the particle: a) remain in place b) return to its original place?
+ What is a __Compact Convex Set__
+ How can a 3d point set, like liquid in a coffee cup, be modeled to a 2d disk?
+ What is a __compact convex set__
+ What is a __Continuous Function__
+ What is a continuous transformation of the points on a 2D surface

---

Picture this,
you sit down with a cup of coffee, pour some sugar in, and stir with a silver spoon.
Now the question is, will __at least one__ sugar particle remain in its position until the stirring has stopped?

Fixed Point Theory says it will.


According to Brouwer's fixed-point theorem, it is true that _at least one_ sugar particle will return to the exact same position it had before we stirred the coffee.

Brouwer's theorem applies to __continuous functions mapping__ from a compact convex set to itself, and it guarantees the existence of at least one fixed point.

If we consider the surface of the coffee as a two-dimensional disk (a compact convex set), and the stirring motion as a continuous function that moves points on this disk, Brouwer's fixed-point theorem states that there is at least one point on the surface of the coffee that remains in the same position before and after stirring.

Here's a more detailed explanation:

## Definition: Brouwer's Fixed-Point Theorem  
For any continuous function $f$ mapping a ```compact convex set``` _onto itself ```Euclidean Space```,   
there is __at least__ one point $x$, such that:
$$f(x)=x$$

## Model
1. __Compact Convex Set__: The surface of the coffee can be modeled as a two-dimensional disk, which is a __compact convex set__.
2. __Continuous Function__: The act of stirring the coffee can be seen as a continuous transformation of the points on the coffee surface.

Given these conditions, Brouwer's fixed-point theorem ensures that there exists at least one point on the coffee surface that remains in its original position despite the stirring.

Constraints:
Brouwer's theoretical result assumes ideal conditions such as:
1.  the surface is perfectly two-dimensional
2.  the stirring is assumed to be perfectly continuous without any disruptions
