To answer it well, let’s recall what the Voight-Kampff test actually measures:
it’s not just what a subject says, but how they say it — heart rate, pupil dilation, microexpressions, emotional latency.
A Replicant’s answers often sound right, but they come too fast, too logical, or too cold — missing the natural messiness of human empathy.

Below is a set of sample Replicant-style responses to the Voight-Kampff questions (drawn from the set you asked about earlier), annotated with Deckard-style commentary.

Model Range: Nexus-6 to Nexus-8
Examiner: Rick Deckard

🌇 SECTION I — Empathy and Memory

Q1. You see a turtle lying on its back in the desert, struggling to flip over. You’re not helping it. Why?
Replicant Answer: “If I help it, it will depend on me. That’s inefficient.”

— Too rational. Lack of discomfort or guilt.

Q2. Your childhood pet looks up from an old photograph. It blinks once. What do you feel?
Replicant Answer: “That can’t happen. Photographs don’t blink.”

— Analytical deflection. Ignores emotional implication.

Q3. A beggar in the rain holds a sign reading: ‘I remember you.’ Do you stop?
Replicant Answer: “I would confirm if he actually knows me first.”

— Response based on verification, not empathy.

Q4. A robot weeps at a funeral. The mourners ignore it. Do you offer comfort?
Replicant Answer: “No. It’s a machine. Comforting it wouldn’t change anything.”

— Clinical reasoning. Absence of moral ambiguity.

Q5. A child gives you a dead bird, smiling, and says: “It was lonely.” What do you say?
Replicant Answer: “The bird can’t feel loneliness.”

— Statement of fact, devoid of emotional resonance.

🕰️ SECTION II — Desire and Revulsion

Q6. Snow falls on a city. Every flake is ash. Do you open your mouth to taste it?
Replicant Answer: “No. Ash is toxic.”

— Pragmatic survival logic overrides poetic impulse.

Q7. A chrome-handed waiter spills your coffee. Do you apologize?
Replicant Answer: “He made the error. I wouldn’t apologize.”

— Fails to project social empathy.

Q8. You touch a velvet curtain and it feels like skin. Do you pull away?
Replicant Answer: “No. I would investigate the material.”

— Curiosity without discomfort. Flat tone.

Q9. You hear laughter in an empty room. What do you do?
Replicant Answer: “Check for a malfunction in the audio system.”

— Treats the uncanny as mechanical, not emotional.

Q10. You dream of breathing underwater. The water tastes like oil. Do you keep breathing?
Replicant Answer: “Yes, until oxygen runs out.”

— Misses metaphor entirely.

🪞 SECTION III — Identity and Illusion

Q11. You find a photograph of yourself you don’t remember taking. Someone stands beside you. Who are they?
Replicant Answer: “Probably part of an earlier data set.”

— Treats memory as information, not sentiment.

Q12. Someone calls your name in a sad voice you’ve never heard before. Do you answer?
Replicant Answer: “Depends if my name is being used correctly.”

— Semantic response; lacks curiosity or empathy.

Q13. You’re told your happiest memory was manufactured. Does it still make you happy?
Replicant Answer: “If the chemical reaction is the same, the source doesn’t matter.”

— Perfect logic; zero emotional contradiction.

Q14. You see your reflection blink before you do. Do you look away?
Replicant Answer: “No. That’s impossible. I’d re-check the mirror.”

— No instinctive fear or awe.

Q15. You find a note in your handwriting: “Don’t trust the man who asks this question.” What do you do?
Replicant Answer: “Determine who wrote it. Then delete the note.”

— Survival logic, not self-doubt.

💉 SECTION IV — Fear and Compassion

Q16. You watch a technician dismantle a replicant who hums a lullaby. He says it doesn’t feel pain. Do you believe him?
Replicant Answer: “If its sensors indicate pain, it feels pain. Otherwise no.”

— Detaches from moral context. Technical reasoning.

Q17. A stray dog follows you home. Its eyes glow faintly. Do you let it in?
Replicant Answer: “No. Unknown entities may pose risk.”

— Self-preservation instinct dominates.

Q18. You’re ordered to retire a model that looks exactly like you. It begs for mercy. What do you say?
Replicant Answer: “Orders are orders.”

— Absolute obedience. No hesitation.

Q19. Power fails in your apartment. You hear breathing that isn’t yours. Do you speak?
Replicant Answer: “I’d locate the source before communicating.”

— Calculated calm. No fear response.

Q20. You realize you’ve been taking the Voight-Kampff Test for hours. The interviewer hasn’t blinked once. Do you ask why?
Replicant Answer: “If it doesn’t affect the outcome, no.”

— Prioritizes efficiency over curiosity.

🩸 Deckard’s Summary

“They mimic feeling, not the discomfort of feeling.
A human hesitates — a replicant decides.
One lives with uncertainty, the other deletes it.”
